# Rebuttal & revision response

## Triage first
Bucket each reviewer point:
- **Fatal if true:** must answer with evidence, new experiment, or concession.
- **Fixable defect:** promise and make concrete edit.
- **Misread:** clarify without blaming the reviewer.
- **Preference/scope:** explain venue-fit and constraints.
- **Incorrect:** refute with citation/data, calmly.
- **Nice-to-have:** accept if cheap, defer if costly.

## Response rules
1. Lead with gratitude only once; spend words on substance.
2. Answer the strongest concern first, especially if multiple reviewers converge.
3. Use exact evidence: table/figure/line numbers, new result, citation, or quote
   from the manuscript.
4. Never say "we will clarify" when the concern is about missing evidence.
5. For new experiments, state protocol and result compactly; do not overclaim.
6. For limitations, concede scope and say how the paper text changes.
7. Keep tone calm: "We agree", "We have added", "This was unclear", "The result
   does not rely on...".

## Revision-plan logic
- In rebuttal-limited venues, prioritize perception-changing answers over minor
  edits.
- In journal revisions, produce a reviewer-by-reviewer response table and a
  change log.
- If a reviewer is wrong because of a paper ambiguity, treat it as your defect.

## Output
- Reviewer-point table: `comment | bucket | response strategy | needed action`.
- Ranked rescue plan: must-answer, should-answer, optional.
- Draft rebuttal text in concise, venue-appropriate language.
- Revision checklist with manuscript locations to edit.
